# Keys, fonts and layout

- **Copy**: drag over the output, double-click a word or triple-click a line, then **Ctrl+C**.
- **Links** in the output underline when you point at them; **shift-click** opens one in your browser.
- **The numpad** walks, once switched on in [Options → Keyboard](options:keyboard): 8 north, 2 south, 7 north-west and so on, 5 looks and searches, `+` and `-` go up and down. Every key can be set to any command, or several separated by `;`. `/numpad off` and `/numpad on` switch it from the command box, so an alias or a trigger can switch it too -- off when a shop asks what you want to buy, say. `/numpad` alone switches it, and `on` goes back to however you last had it on. Holding a key sends once, unless **Holding a key** is set to repeat: then it keeps sending, twice or four times a second, until you let go.
- **Your commands**: ↑ and ↓ go back through what you have typed. **Ctrl+↑**, or **History** at the end of the command box, lists the newest 50 with a search box that reaches all of them: type part of one, then click it (or pick it with ↓ and Enter) to put it in the box, ready to change or send. The last 500 are kept in this browser -- but nothing typed before MIP is live, so a password typed at 3K's own login is never kept.
- **Keep the last command** in the box, also under Keyboard, to send it again with Enter.
- **Speedwalk**, also under Keyboard: `.3n2e` walks n, n, n, e, e, as zMUD does; `h` `j` `k` `l` are nw, ne, sw, se.
- [Options → Fonts](options:fonts) sets the output's font, size and spacing. **Ctrl +** and **Ctrl -** make everything else bigger or smaller.
- [Options → Colours](options:colours) chooses the terminal's colour scheme -- Dank, Linux Tango, Solarized, Dracula, Nord and more, or your own from a file (Alacritty, iTerm2, Ghostty, Windows Terminal or Xresources, as terminalcolors.com offers them) -- and whether the rest of the window is **Dark**, **Light**, or **matches the terminal**.
- [Options → Layout](options:panels) shows and hides the panels, and the vitals: any of HP, SP, GP1, GP2, the enemy (shown last, only while you fight) and either of 3K's two guild lines (side by side under the strip, as 3K sends them), or the whole strip -- which can also sit under the command box instead of above it.
- **Click a gauge's name** -- HP, SP, GP1, GP2 -- to call it something else: `PU` and `SPU` rather than whatever 3K names your guild's points. The name is kept with the character, because the next character is in another guild, and it beats the one the MUD sends. Clear it to go back to the MUD's.
